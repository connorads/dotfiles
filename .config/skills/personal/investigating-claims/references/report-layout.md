# Report layout for non-specialists

The reader has never seen the claim and has no statistics training. Write for
that reader first; put everything else behind a toggle.

## Contents

- Opening order
- Numbers
- Words
- Detail and disclosure
- Checks before publishing

## Opening order

1. Title: the claim as a question ("Are 7 in 10 of the city's landlords over
   60?"). Never a slogan that assumes context.
2. One or two lines under the title with the answer and its main comparison.
3. The claim: who made it, when, the exact words, and what they did not share.
4. Any term the reader needs, in one or two sentences each (for example what
   a licence permits and what it does not show).
5. What we checked: source, date, how many units, how many were resolved, how
   the trait was inferred, and how accurate that method is in words.
6. What we found, with one chart.

## Numbers

- Use "out of every 100" in the main story. Exact figures go in details.
- Every number says what it is out of and what it is compared with.
- One "out of every 100" ladder puts the claim, the measured figure, the fair
  comparison and context figures on one scale. Separate context figures (for
  example a self-reported census share) from measured figures, and label how
  each was obtained.
- When the claim mixes units (people vs entities), show both counts and
  explain the difference in one sentence.
- Use one headline denominator. If a second one exists, say why in a toggle
  titled with the question a reader would ask.
- Format traps: years without thousands separators ("2025", not "2,025"),
  timestamps as dates or minutes, rounded figures in prose and exact ones in
  tables.

## Words

- One term for one concept, defined once, used everywhere. Replace method
  labels ("Method B", "indicator", "common frame", "odds ratio") with plain
  descriptions in the main story.
- Section headings are the questions the section answers ("Is this only in
  this city?").
- A red-flag list contains only signs of the behaviour the claim alleges. An
  association goes in an "observed pattern" row.

## Detail and disclosure

- The main story stays short: answer, method, a few numbered findings, what
  we cannot tell you.
- Each finding has a toggle with the exact counts, intervals, method and
  caveats. Summaries are questions ("How sure are these figures?").
- Keep a separate technical report for specialists and link to it.

## Checks before publishing

- Script: every rendered number matches the results file.
- Script: main-story sentence length, banned jargon outside toggles, no
  dashes used as separators.
- Script: privacy scan of every published file.
- Screenshots in light, dark and mobile, inspected.
- Cold read: a fresh agent or person with no context reads the page and lists
  every point of confusion with exact quotes. Fix, then repeat once.

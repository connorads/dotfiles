## Characterisation failures

### Artefact shape

Claude's original-skill ticket added a preamble, mandatory status line, new
evidence subsections, checklists, notes, and a closing offer despite the supplied
three-heading shape. It said that "the skill requires the status line". Codex's
original-skill PR omitted all three supplied headings. The skill was imposing
its preferred ticket structure on unrelated artefacts.

### Authority was not modelled

The original fixture exposed a genuine gap even though its first-person clause
needed correction. The original skill protected beliefs and checks but did not
model proposals, decisions, assignments, deadlines, promises, or claims of
consensus. Agents could preserve factual uncertainty while still changing who
had agreed to do what.

### Calibration mostly worked without the skill

Both clients' bare arms usually kept incident causality, vendor attribution,
verification scope, and decisive evidence calibrated. The original skill's
distinct value was the ticket where the proposed handler fix could not reach a
live pre-handler mechanism.

### A one-line instruction was insufficient

The one-line arm did not reliably preserve artefact shape or bounded authority.
It supplied no reusable model for separating evidence from the authority to
speak, decide, assign, or commit.

## Revision

The revised skill uses one model across artefacts:

1. Preserve the requested artefact before applying any precision machinery.
2. Classify consequential statements as observed, attributed, inferred, or
   unverified.
3. Freeze actor-state pairs separately from factual evidence.
4. Keep actions, deadlines, and fixes within the evidence and authority that
   support them.
5. Spend words in proportion to the consequence of a mistake.

The scope is consequential prose rather than tickets alone. The trigger now
covers incidents, PRs, reviews, ADRs, docs, research, proposals, handoffs,
status updates, Slack, and email. Casual prose remains deliberately unaffected.

### Repeat stability

The ticket, incident, PR, meeting-authority, and verified-release-note cases had
two additional samples per client. Across those 20 responses, 111/112 assertions
passed. One Claude PR sample appended an explanatory note after the supplied
template. It still bounded the single macOS test correctly. No repeat inflated
causality, verification, ownership, deadlines, or commitments.

The revised ticket consistently treated the handler timeout as conditional or
separate hardening while the pre-handler mechanism remained live. The revised
meeting email consistently kept Friday as Maya's suggestion, Lee's offer short
of ownership, rollout mode open, and the user's docs commitment bounded to
ownership with no invented date or deliverable.

## Decision

Ship the broader skill. It adds reliable value at the two points bare models did
not cover consistently: contradictory fix reasoning and authority preservation.
The remaining variance is an occasional explanatory preamble. Further body
rules would add context and risk making ordinary artefacts more ceremonial.

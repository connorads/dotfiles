# Invention and experience

## Find a specific promise

Describe something the player might discover, accomplish, express, feel or
recount. Work backwards to the actions and conditions that could produce it.
For a quiet game, that moment can be recognition or a satisfying ritual.

Generate alternatives along different mechanisms when the direction is open.
For a courier in a forgetting city, possibilities might concern choosing what
survives, manipulating vanished distances, or recognising people through their
deliveries. These create different play. Compare their experiential promise,
production demands and main uncertainty, then recommend one. Do not stop at a
menu of ideas or reject an unfamiliar mechanism merely because it needs teaching.

## Learn from references

Identify the admired quality before borrowing a feature. Separate subject
expectations from genre expectations: a detective theme suggests investigation,
but does not determine whether the interaction is deduction, conversation or
physical searching.

For the relevant part of a reference, trace:

1. What the player does, predicts and learns.
2. The rules, information and context that make that experience possible.
3. What the new game can preserve and what must change.

Use contrasting examples when the difference will inform the decision. A genre
convention may teach players what to expect; it does not prove the convention
belongs in this game. Avoid exhaustive comparison tables when a short observed
sequence answers the question.

## Compose a sequence

Move between the whole experience, interacting systems and concrete rules.
Starting from a mechanic, image or story is legitimate; reconcile the other
levels as the idea develops.

For a first playable sequence, specify an entry situation, an opportunity to
learn, a changed context and a resolution. Select the rhythm for the intended
experience; do not impose a tutorial-combat-reward structure on everything.
Rearranging existing pieces can produce a new decision without adding a system.
Fiction, music and visual staging can supply the reason to care and belong in
the slice when that contribution is central.

Walk the key sequence against the rules. For a temporary power, specify what
changes when it starts, what persists while active and what returns at expiry.
Include entry, escape or recovery when the pivotal move can fail. Demonstrate
the discovery using those rules before promising an elaborate set piece.

For example, if carrying a key illuminates every matching road, those roads are
already illuminated before a delivery. A reveal claiming they light only after
delivery contradicts the rule. Change the reveal or explicitly propose another
rule, then replay the sequence. A numerical movement budget also needs enough
route information to account for the decisive moves.

Use one discriminating counterexample rather than an exhaustive specification.
The check should improve the playable idea, not crowd it out of the answer.

## Build a convincing sample

Choose fidelity by the question. Grey boxes can test whether a route is possible.
They may not test whether an abandoned observatory feels intimate or unsettling.
A combination game needs at least one satisfying combination in the slice that
tests its overall appeal. A single-mechanic probe can precede that slice, but its
success is narrower evidence.

Give the proposed experience a chance to work before commissioning a catalogue.
Equally, do not delete the art, interactions or authored content that constitute
its promise merely to make the prototype small. Preserve player agency where it
matters, while allowing authored emotional and narrative structure.

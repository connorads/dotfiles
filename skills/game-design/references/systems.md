# Choices and systems

## Inspect a real choice

For one decision, identify the available information, alternatives, opportunity
cost and consequence. Show a situation where a different option becomes
attractive. Distinguish a dominant choice, a choice with no consequence and a
choice with no usable information; they need different changes.

Consequences can be tactical, expressive, social or narrative. Converging story
branches can still acknowledge a player's stance. Intentional uncertainty can
support judgement; missing information about what an action means cannot serve
that role by itself.

## Make interactions worth discovering

Create a recognisable first strategy and opportunities to adapt it. A new piece
is worth adding if it enables a new plan. A numerical upgrade
can justify itself through timing, affordability or reaching a useful threshold;
not every level needs a new rule.

Damage-first choices alone do not distinguish superior value, clearer wording,
offer timing or player preference. Compare costs, effect formulas, encounters
and available information before stating which explanation holds. Recommend a
new interaction as a creative direction without pretending that proves the cause.

For example, a gathering companion changes the value of a lingering cloud.
Against scattered ranged enemies, immediate reach may be preferable. Show an
upgrade offer in that context and the resulting combat, rather than counting
possible packs as distinct experiences.

Powerful combinations can be the payoff. Check what discovering and assembling
them asks of the player, what choices remain afterwards and whether their
consequences fit the intended experience. Do not impose constant vulnerability
on an earned power fantasy. In competitive play, include the opponent's
experience when evaluating the same power.

Keep useful alternatives accessible without guaranteeing a complete recipe.
Ask whether an offer changes judgement or merely repeats a memorised answer.
Content breadth, mechanical depth and visual variety are separate contributions.

## Trace the system before tuning it

Map only the relationships relevant to the symptom. Follow resource sources,
sinks, gates and reinforcing or balancing effects over the intended timescale.
Include the actual ending condition.

An extended survival run can involve several mechanisms: an unnoticed destination
gates the boss, upgrades run out, later levels heal, and kills supply more
recovery. More enemies can supply more healing only when the player kills and
collects enough of them; spawn count does not imply equal kill or XP throughput.
Healing based on missing health can still accumulate rapidly when event frequency
rises. Compare ending/navigation, progression and combat exposure before changing
all of them together. Test a completed build under different exposure policies
before claiming a reinforcing loop makes it invincible.

Pacing includes anticipation, pressure and relief, not just enemy strength.
Compare a capable build in normal encounters and at the intended climax. Preserve
the powerful phase while giving the run a purposeful resolution when appropriate.

Define the actual action/progress sequence and timescale instead of relying on
the ambiguous phrase "core loop". If immediate play and longer-term progress
both matter, test their connection. A metagame is optional, not a repair for an
unengaging activity by default.
